Shap, Cumbria

The last postal round in Shap

Until 1976 a postman walked the post out of Shap every morning: over the fields to Shap Abbey, up to isolated Tailbert, down into Swindale and over the fell to Wet Sleddale. Now you can walk or run the round yourself.

Where the story comes from

Whitehaven author Alan Cleaver spent five years tracing the routes rural postmen and women walked, from the penny post of the 1840s until vans replaced them in the late 1960s and 1970s. His book follows those paths from the Outer Hebrides to the Cornish coast, and gathers the stories of the posties who walked them.

These were long days. Rounds of 10 to 20 miles were common, walked six days a week in every weather, often by the same person for most of their working life. Where the road took too long, postmen made their own shortcuts. Many are still marked on maps as postman’s paths.

Shap is one of the Cumbrian rounds in the book. Stuart Lewis walked its 15 miles until 1976, carrying a leather pouch to sell stamps and postal orders at the farms. Before him, in the 1950s, the round belonged to Jimmy Eland.

The round

A circular route out of Shap and back, following Alan Cleaver’s mapping of the round. Start and finish at Birchwood Cafe, run by the family of one of the last postmen to walk it.

Sketch map, not to scale. Navigate with OS Explorer OL5 and a compass.

  1. Birchwood Cafe

    Start on Main Street. Head north out of the village and take the lane west towards Shap Abbey.

  2. Shap Abbey

    Cross the Lowther by the wooden-sided bridge. From the abbey, take the footpath west over the fields, which can be very wet, until you meet the concrete water-board road.

  3. Tailbert

    Follow the road south, then west to the isolated farm at Tailbert. Take a bearing south-west and drop into Swindale.

  4. Truss Gap, Swindale

    Follow the valley road to Truss Gap Farm. The postman sometimes went on to Swindale Head and Mosedale Cottage when there was post for them.

  5. Over Glede Howe

    Cross Swindale Beck and climb the fell. This is the true postman’s path: his own shortcut, once marked with wooden staves to find the driest line, even in snow. The staves are long gone, so take a bearing.

  6. Sleddale Hall

    Drop south off the fell to the farmhouse made famous by the film Withnail and I.

  7. Thorney Bank postbox

    Follow the path east above Wet Sleddale to Thorney Bank and the Victorian postbox the postman emptied on his round.

  8. Back to Birchwood Cafe

    Take the road east to the A6 and turn north back into Shap. Finish where you started, then log your round.

Before you go. This is a long, rough day with pathless, boggy sections. Mountain Rescue advise carrying a map and compass, and knowing how to use them, even if you have GPS. Check the forecast, tell someone your plan, and close every gate behind you.

Walk responsibly

Much of the route crosses working farmland. Keep dogs on leads near stock, use gates and stiles, and leave no trace.
